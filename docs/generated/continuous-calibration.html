Steadiness gate

Today's calibration is one invisible snapshot taken the instant a player taps the grip button — any noise in that instant is baked into the whole throw. This page walks through a proposed replacement: a reference that keeps tracking while the phone is still, and freezes the moment the player starts to aim.

1 · Where the noise gets locked in

Same throw, two mechanisms. The difference is not what the player does — it's when the reference orientation stops listening.

Today

Tap → single-sample snapshot → armed

Proposed

Settling ⇄ Aiming → Swinging → Release

2 · Try the mechanic

No orientation sensor on a desktop, so drag inside the square to stand in for phone tilt. Hold near centre to stay Settling; drag out past the small dead zone to lock into Aiming; drag hard and fast toward an edge to trigger Swinging. The gold ring on the phone marks the instant a haptic tick would fire — the Settling→Aiming lock.

3 · Seven smaller intuitiveness fixes

Surfaced alongside the calibration redesign — each closes a gap between what the sensors are doing and what the player can see or feel.

D2

On-phone live tilt indicator

A level bubble during Aiming, on the screen the player is actually looking at — not only the TV across the room.

D3

Three-state status badge

Extends the grip button's existing label swap (TAP TO AIM / SWING!) with a middle "Settling…" state, instead of new UI.

D4

Haptic pulses on lock & release

The Vibration API is unused today. One tick on Settling→Aiming, a distinct one on release — eyes-free confirmation for near-zero cost.

D5

First-throw TV mirror diagram

A small live phone-tilt → stone-tilt diagram on the TV, shown only for a player's first throw — cause and effect learned in real play, not a slide.

D6

Explicit practice toss

Reframes the existing disarm/re-arm path as a sanctioned warm-up before a player's first scored throw, not just an error-recovery move.

D7

Visible steady-hold gate

A fill-ring on the phone for the existing invisible armGraceMs guard — the hold requirement becomes something the player can watch happen.

D8

Post-throw numeric recap

"swing: fast · release: 12° low · light spin" on the phone, pairing the TV's pass/fail with the inputs that produced it.
