Rock Skipping — Player Onboarding

Tap. Swing. Stop.
That's the whole throw.

Your phone is the stone. This page walks through arming the throw, why the tap matters, and the two things that actually decide whether your rock skips clean across the lake or dies on the first bounce.

① TAP TO AIM → ② SWING → ③ STOP DEAD
01 — The Gesture

There is no throw button

Only three moments matter, and the game reads all three straight off the phone's sensors — nothing is inferred or averaged in between.

STEP 1

Tap to arm

Hold the phone flat, like the stone itself, pointed at the TV — then tap the button. The tap captures exactly how you're holding the phone right now as the neutral, "flat and facing forward" reference. Everything from here is measured against that instant.

STEP 2

Swing like you mean it

A real arm swing, not a wrist wave — the game measures peak hand speed through the whole motion. A flick of the wrist as you come through is what puts spin on the stone.

STEP 3

Stop dead — that's release

There's no button to let go of. The sudden stop is the throw. Wherever the phone is tilted and pointed the instant it stops is the stone's aim and face angle — so finish your swing pointed where you want the stone to go.

02 — Calibration

The tap is the calibration

There's no separate calibration screen. Arming the throw and setting your "zero" happen in the same tap — which is why how you hold the phone at that moment matters.

Point at the TV, hold it flat, then tap.

That pose becomes "flat, facing forward." Every tilt and turn afterward is measured as a delta from it, not from some fixed absolute direction.

Tapped pointing the wrong way? Tap again.

A second tap stands down without throwing, so you can re-aim and tap once more to recalibrate from scratch — no reload, no penalty.

Recentre anytime between throws.

If your "flat" has drifted — you sat down, changed grip — use Recentre to capture a fresh reference before you arm the next throw.

04 — Cheat Sheet

What each throw actually looked like

💪 Strong & far

  • Fast, full-arm swing — the speed dial is the only thing that buys distance
  • Face angle near the sweet spot, roughly level
  • A real wrist snap through the stop, keeping the stone stable bounce to bounce

🫳 Short

  • Slow, gentle swing — not enough speed for the energy budget
  • Angle and spin can be perfect and it still won't travel far
  • Below the minimum swing speed, the throw doesn't even register — stay armed and try again

💧 Sinks fast

  • Face too flat or too steep at the exact instant you stopped
  • No wrist flick — the stone tumbles instead of holding its face
  • Can happen on an otherwise hard, strong swing — speed alone doesn't save it

🚫 Misfire

  • Swing was too weak to count as a throw at all
  • You stay armed — no re-tap needed, just swing again
  • Tapping again instead cancels and lets you recalibrate

Aim is wherever the phone points the instant it stops — not where you're looking. Finish your swing facing the target.
