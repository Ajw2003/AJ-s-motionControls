RockSkipping · documentation architecture · 8 Sep 2026

Five tiers, each one narrower and faster than the last

A couch party game where the PC drives the TV and phones are motion controllers. Its documentation was rebuilt on 8 September 2026 after an audit found twenty broken links and four tables describing a certificate authority that no longer exists. This is the shape it took.

5 tiers 12 live documents 1,636 lines in the stack 0 broken links

The stack, drawn as a skip

The tiers are not five equal folders. Each one asks a narrower question than the one above it and is rewritten more often — which, in a game about skipping stones, draws itself.

How a change moves through it

Reading runs downward; updates run back up, and they stop early. Work changes today's plan constantly and the definition of done almost never — which is the whole reason the two were split apart.

What each tier holds

Tiers 2 and 4 were scoped by choice rather than convention: 10% milestone bands, and seven system documents. Both decisions and their reasoning are recorded below.

02

Roadmap

docs/Roadmap.md

What does 0% to 100% actually mean?

100% is one sentence: a person who has never seen the repository downloads one file, runs it, and hosts a party — with no internet, no router, no configuration and no instructions. Their guests scan a QR code and play.

  • Ten milestones at 10% bands, each with an acceptance criterion someone else could check — an observable outcome, not a feeling that the work is finished.
  • An explicit statement of what 100% is not: no store pages, no localisation, no online play, Windows only.

Why 10% and not 1%: ten bands put a milestone at one to two weeks of real work. Finer bands were considered and rejected — at 1% this becomes a backlog that needs re-cutting every time priorities move, and this project reverses hosting and physics decisions often enough that it would need re-cutting constantly.

04

Systems

docs/systems/

How does this actually work, and what must I not break?

Seven runtime-critical systems — if one is wrong, the game does not work. Every document carries the same four sections: what it owns, how it works, invariants, and traps that have already cost someone a day.

Event systemPub/sub across four scenes. Subscription order is load-bearing
NetworkingOne relay socket, one hop. Flat DTOs — JsonUtility can't nest
Motion pipelineQuaternions only. The play pose sits on a gimbal singularity
Skip physicsStep is pure. The preview runs it hundreds of times a frame
Scoring & turnsPush-based. Only ScoreManager knows point values
Hosting & certsThe PC is the network. DNS must hold :53 before ICS
Rocks & tuningA rock owns three numbers. A global never lives on a rock

Why seven and not twelve. Scope stops where a document still gets maintained. Audio was left out because it is paused on a decision, and distribution because ZeroSetupPlan.md already covers it accurately — and adding a document that duplicates a live one is how two documents start disagreeing.

Two things that hang off the stack

Neither is a tier. Both are indexed from tier 1 so they can be found, and neither is ever built against.

docs/archive/

Frozen · 11 files · 2,547 lines

Documents that were correct when written and are now inert: shipped plans, fixed bugs, and one insurance policy against a disaster that happened and was answered a different way. Nothing in it describes current behaviour.

They are kept because the reasoning is often the only record of why something is the way it is — and because deleting documents that other documents point at is exactly what broke the link graph in the first place.

docs/grant/

Parallel · 11 files · 1,363 lines

The funding case for the plugin the game turned out to contain — the offline secure-context work, extracted. A different reader and a different purpose from everything else in the folder.

Explicitly not the project plan. Its week-one work list was doing that job by accident; tiers 2 and 3 own it now.

Four conventions that keep it from decaying again

Each one is a direct response to something the 8 September audit found.

01

Cite claims to file:line

Most assertions link to the code they describe. This is what made the audit mechanical instead of a matter of opinion — every certificate claim could be checked against MagicDomain.cs in seconds.

02

Say what a measurement cannot show

The findings documents that state their own limits are the reason a ten-round hardware dead end is not being reopened on a hunch. A tool that cannot fail is worse than no tool.

03

When a decision reverses, fix the body — not just the header

A "Superseded — see X" box stacked on top of a table that still says the old thing is how four documents ended up describing a certificate authority that had ceased to resolve. The reader trusts the table.

04

An inert document moves to archive/ — it is not deleted

Deleting 34 documents that other documents pointed at is what broke twenty links. The fix is to move things and index them, so a stale pointer lands somewhere that explains itself.
