Ten milestones
from nothing to a stranger playing

A couch party game: the PC drives the TV, phones are motion controllers over a web page, no app to install. 100% is one sentence — someone who has never seen the repository downloads one file, runs it, and hosts a party with no internet, no router and no instructions. Everything below is on the way to exactly that.

Why ten, and why 10% each

Ten bands put one milestone at roughly one to two weeks of real work — coarse enough to be a meaningful unit, fine enough that "where am I" has an answer better than "somewhere in the middle". Finer bands were considered and rejected: at 1% this becomes a backlog that needs re-cutting every time priorities move, and this project reverses hosting and physics decisions often enough that it would need re-cutting constantly. Every milestone below carries an acceptance criterion someone else could check — an observable outcome, not a feeling that the work is finished.

10%

Infrastructure spike

Complete

The load-bearing scaffolding, before any of it was worth building on: the event system ported and made multi-scene safe, the four-scene structure, the Node relay written and hardened with its own tests.

AcceptanceEvent-system verification proves publish/subscribe survives a scene load and that destroyed subscribers are dropped; npm test passes in relay/.
20%

Motion pipeline

Complete

The phone's orientation arrives in Unity as the same rotation the player is holding. Tilt the phone ten degrees, the stone tilts ten degrees.

AcceptanceThat correspondence holds on both iOS and Android, in the pose the game is actually played in — phone upright, screen facing the player, aimed at a television.

It is its own milestone because that pose sits exactly on a gimbal singularity. DeviceOrientationEvent reports Tait-Bryan angles where beta = 90° is "upright, screen facing you" — so the grip reference sits on the singularity, not near it. Sensor noise alone swung aim by tens of degrees. The fix was to stop decomposing entirely: the phone differences two quaternions and sends four floats, and Unity applies them to the stone verbatim.

30%

Skip physics

Complete

A stone thrown at water skips, sinks, or fails — and the throw is what decides which. Shown in the clip at the top of this page.

AcceptanceA speed/angle/roll sweep in which a better throw beats a worse one, every run satisfying the energy invariant, and two different throws producing visibly different results with no tuning edit between them.
  • A bounce is one energy budget. It takes a loss, then splits what survives between forward and upward motion — it never synthesises lift out of horizontal speed. An earlier version did, and stones gained energy every bounce and skipped for minutes on end.
  • Spin is the stabiliser. It holds the stone's face steady through the slap and decays every bounce, so a run always ends. Without it the stone tumbles and dies in two bounces — which is what an unspun stone actually does.
  • The solver is pure. No events, no scene, no side effects — which is the only reason the aim preview can run it speculatively without scoring imaginary throws.

It is not "physics works". An earlier model had 25 tuning fields fighting each other, and a one-point tuning edit moved the result more than the player's own throw did. The milestone is the throw mattering.

40%

Turns, scoring, multiplayer

Complete

A real match, with real people, to a real result: the roster, match phases, turn rotation, push-based scoring, late join, names and colours, the results screen.

AcceptanceA cross-platform session with five or more phones — iOS and Android together — completes a full match, with a player joining mid-lobby or dropping and returning handled without a restart.
50%

Local hosting and secure context

Complete

The PC hosts the game itself. No cloud service is involved in any way — the relay runs as a child process of the game, over HTTPS, with a certificate obtained and renewed with no human involved.

AcceptancePress Play; a phone opens the join URL, gets a valid certificate, and its motion sensors unlock. Nothing was configured first and no cloud account exists.

This is half the project. Phone motion sensors are gated on a secure context — without this milestone there is no game, only a demo that works on the developer's machine. The chain is unforgiving: sensors need a secure context, a secure context needs a certificate, a certificate needs a resolvable public name, and a living room has none of it.

The public service this originally leaned on died at the DNS layer — not degraded, gone. It was replaced by a single-name certificate on an owned domain, renewed unattended by a scheduled job and published where a fresh install can fetch it.

60%

Offline hosting

Complete

The living room has no internet. The game does not care. The PC raises its own Wi-Fi network, hands out addresses, answers DNS for its own hostname, and isolates its guests.

AcceptanceUnplug everything. Start the game. A phone that has never seen this network joins by QR and plays a full match.

It is separate from milestone 5 because that one needs a working network and this one needs the PC to be the network. Three genuine blockers each looked like a Windows limitation and were not — including a Wi-Fi radio that must be on but need not be associated, which reports failure in a way that reads exactly like "Windows cannot do this offline".

70%

Frictionless join

Complete

Joining stops being a ritual. A guest used to pay two camera scans and about nine interactions — and pay all nine again at every session, forever.

AcceptanceA guest who played last week arrives, scans once, and is in. A guest who is stuck is visible on the TV as stuck, at the stage they are stuck at.

Closed after ten rounds of hardware testing. The captive portal was abandoned deliberately, not forgotten: Android resolves both probe hosts every 60 seconds and issues no HTTP probe, while the same phone's browser completes a full request to the same port seconds later. Ten rounds excluded every host-side cause by direct measurement.

80%

Rock variety and selection

Built, not done

Six stones that feel different, and a way to choose one that is worth doing. Players pick by pointing the phone at the shore on the TV, then confirming on the handset.

AcceptanceSix rocks are selectable, each plays measurably differently, a player picks one by pointing their phone at the shore, and the pick survives into their throw.

This reads as nearly finished, and it is not

Every piece of machinery exists — the catalog, the shore UI, the wire format, a host settings panel, in-play telemetry and an offline sweep to compare rocks against each other. But all six tunings ship identical values: SpinMultiplier: 1, VerticalMultiplier: 1, HorizontalMultiplier: 1.

That was deliberate — the physics was being judged bare first, so that every difference between two throws was the throw. But it means the acceptance criterion above cannot be met by any amount of further code. Boulder and Shard are promised opposite personalities in writing and are, today, the same stone in a different silhouette. Six rocks that skip identically is a worse experience than one rock, because it invites a choice that does not matter.

What is left is authoring: deciding what a Skimmer is versus a Shard and putting numbers on it. The tool that checks it already sweeps per rock.

90%

Presentation

Partial · blocked

It stops looking like a prototype: audio, trails, water response, camera framing, music.

AcceptanceSomeone watching a match from the couch, who has not been told what to look for, does not notice anything missing.

Built: the skip pitch ladder, sink, fail, score and win sounds, trails, water ripples, the render-pipeline migration, arc colour and ghost glow. Not built: music, and the scheduler that stops same-frame sounds colliding.

The blocker is a decision, not code

Where source clips come from — licensed pack, commissioned, or generated — is unanswered, and has been long enough that the milestone has gone as far as it can without it. Everything shipping today is procedurally generated at build time, which is the only reason it got this far.

100%

Zero-setup distribution

Not started

The sentence at the top of this page. Bundling the runtime and the relay inside the build, getting the defaults right with no override files, and an installer that is compiled and signed.

AcceptanceA person who has never seen the repository is handed one file. They install it, run it, and host a party. You are not in the room and they do not phone you.

Four blockers, known and unchanged. The third is the one to attack first — the relay and controller folders are not in the build at all. They live outside the Unity project, so the build never copies them, and a downloaded build fails there before it reaches the bundled-runtime or dependency problems. Everything else in this milestone is downstream of fixing it.
